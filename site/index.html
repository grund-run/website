Production on your own servers.

grund turns two to ten dedicated servers into a production platform. Databases you can restore to any second, deploys that roll back on their own, and evidence that all of it works. If we ever disappear, you keep a standard cluster in your own git.

Follow the build How it works

  • Your servers, not ours.

    Two to ten dedicated machines you rent or own. No per-request markup, no surprise egress bill.

  • Restores you can prove.

    Every night grund restores your database somewhere safe and checks it, so a backup is never just a hope.

  • Deploys that undo themselves.

    A release that fails its health checks rolls back before your users notice, with nobody paged.

  • Leave whenever you like.

    Everything is a standard cluster with manifests in your git. It keeps running without us.

Every guarantee comes with evidence.

A promise you cannot check is a hope. grund tests its own guarantees on a schedule and shows you what it measured.

restore
Point-in-time restore of PostgreSQL, drilled nightly into an isolated space and compared row for row.
failover
Three servers in quorum. An opt-in drill drains one and measures how long recovery takes.
backups
Write-ahead log streamed off-site, never only on the servers it protects.
alerts
Certificate expiry, backup age, disk and replication lag, sent to email or your phone before users notice.

Deploys that roll back on their own.

Releases are promoted by image digest through readiness gates. When health checks fail, the last good release comes back in seconds.

From bare servers to production in three steps.

No platform team, no months of Kubernetes. Bring the servers; grund brings the production layer.

  1. 1

    Point grund at your servers.

    Three fresh dedicated machines become a highly available cluster, with a pinned, tested version of every component.

  2. 2

    Connect your repository.

    grund writes the cluster's desired state into your git. Review it like any other change.

  3. 3

    Deploy, and watch the evidence.

    Ship an image or a Dockerfile. Health gates, rollbacks, backups and drills are on from the first deploy.

This is how grund is designed to work. It is not available yet.

Built for small teams running a real product.

A good fit

  • SaaS founders and product teams of one to ten engineers.
  • Teams leaving a platform bill that grew faster than the product did.
  • Anyone who wants Kubernetes' guarantees without running Kubernetes by hand.

Not what it is for

  • A hobby project on one small server. Simpler tools serve it well.
  • Enterprises that buy through a sales team.
  • Multi-region, or an SLA on your hardware. You own the servers; grund owns the automation.

The cost, up front

Real high availability needs three servers, a quorum. On dedicated hardware from a provider like Hetzner that is roughly €290 a month before storage.

A single-server setup is supported, and labelled honestly as not highly available.
