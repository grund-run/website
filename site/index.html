Guarantees

Every guarantee comes with evidence.

A promise you cannot check is a hope. grund shows the proof next to each one: when it was last tested, and what it measured.

  • Restore your database to any second

    PostgreSQL with its write-ahead log archived off-site, not on the same servers. Every night a drill restores it into an isolated space and compares it.

    You will seeRestore drill 03:12 · RPO 4 s · RTO 3 m 40 s

  • Losing a server doesn't lose the product

    Three servers in quorum, replicas spread across them, and disruption budgets. An opt-in drill drains a server and measures how long recovery takes.

    You will seeCan lose any 1 server · failover drill 2 days ago

  • Deploys never take you down, and roll back on their own

    Releases are promoted by image digest through readiness gates. When health checks fail, the previous release comes back without anyone paging you.

    You will seesha256:7c1e… healthy in 41 s · one-click rollback

  • The platform's own upgrades are safe

    Versioned release channels. Every upgrade is rehearsed on our own clusters first, and an upgrade that fails its gates reverts itself.

    You will seeRehearsed on 6 clusters for 9 days

  • Our control plane cannot root your servers

    An agent on your cluster pulls signed intents and verifies them. The hosted control plane never holds SSH keys and never runs commands on your machines.

    You will seeSigned intents only · no SSH keys held

  • You know before your users do

    Metrics and alert rules out of the box: certificate expiry, backup age, disk space and replication lag, routed to email or your phone.

    You will seeBackup 6 h old · cert 61 d left · disk 42 %

The evidence shown is an example of what grund will report. grund is being built; these are the guarantees it is designed around, not a list of shipped features.

Ownership

Your servers. Your git. You can leave.

grund automates a stack you could run yourself. Nothing about it depends on us staying around.

Everything lives in your git

Desired state is rendered to plain manifests, committed to your repository and applied by Flux. Nothing exists only in our database.

Losing us is survivable

Export gives you a standard k3s, Flux and CloudNativePG setup that keeps running without grund. There is no proprietary runtime underneath.

Secrets stay with you

Encrypted in your git and decrypted inside your cluster. They never sit in a tool's database, ours included.

Who it's for

Built for small teams running a real product.

A good fit

  • SaaS founders and product teams of one to ten engineers.
  • Teams leaving a platform bill that grew faster than the product did.
  • Anyone who wants Kubernetes' guarantees without running Kubernetes by hand.

Not what it is for

  • A hobby project on one small server. Simpler tools serve it well.
  • Enterprises that buy through a sales team.
  • Multi-region, or an SLA on your hardware. You own the servers; grund owns the automation.

The cost, up front. Real high availability needs three servers, a quorum. On dedicated hardware from a provider like Hetzner, that is roughly €290 a month before storage.

A single-server setup is supported, and it is labelled honestly as not highly available.
